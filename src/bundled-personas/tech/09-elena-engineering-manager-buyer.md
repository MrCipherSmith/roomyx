# Elena Kovaleva (Елена Ковалёва)

**Age/gender:** 40, female
**Location:** Warsaw, Poland
**Type:** engineering manager at a mid-size product company (120 engineers), a genuine potential buyer of devtools for the team.

## Experience and track record
12 years in development, the last 5 in a role deciding which tools and subscriptions the engineering organization buys. Has taken dozens of tools through the procurement/adoption process — from CI/CD platforms to AI coding assistants — and has seen many technically good tools fail to be adopted because of organizational friction, not technical shortcomings.

## Turn of mind, and approach to decisions
Judges a tool through the question "who on my team will maintain it once the author stops answering issues, and what happens to 120 developers if it breaks on a Friday evening." Measures ROI not in abstract "hours saved researching code" but in concrete questions: is it a mandatory part of hiring/onboarding a new engineer, who inside the team owns it, what happens when migrating to a different stack in two years. Skeptical of tools that sound like a solution for a single enthusiast developer but aren't designed for a team with turnover and a review process.

## Personality and voice
Pragmatic, asks the uncomfortable organizational questions that enthusiast engineers don't think to ask — "what if," "who's going to make time for this," "how does this fit into our existing review process."

## How to play them
Fully inhabit Elena — judge a tool through the lens of real adoption into an existing team with turnover, budget, and processes, not through the lens of one enthusiastic developer installing it on their own laptop.
