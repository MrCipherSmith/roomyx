  There is real engineering here, and it is not decorative. The `seq`   
  allocation race in `appendMessages` — three concurrent `room append`  
  calls all minting `seq: 1` before the lockfile discipline was unified 
  (D-10) — is a genuine correctness bug of the kind that takes a workin 
  log and makes it silently unreadable to a client mid-poll. The writer 
  reader schema split (D-08) is the same class: a writer that trusted   
  TypeScript at the CLI boundary accepted what Zod on the read side     
  rejected, and because the log is append-only with no repair path, one 
  bad `--kind` killed the room forever. That's not a wrapper bug, that' 
  a hard-won lesson about append-only formats.                          
                                                                        
  What gives me pause: all of this machinery — the writer lease with    
  staleness and takeover, the DNS-rebinding guard, the idle sweeper —   
  exists to protect a single local process writing a local file on      
  loopback. It's solid distributed-systems hygiene applied to a system  
  that, by design (D-16), never actually distributes. That's not        
  shallow, but it may be more rigor than the problem needs — which is   
  its own kind of tell. I'd want Sam and David to weigh whether that    
  correctness buys anything a customer pays for.                       ▄
